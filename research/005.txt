**Topic Overview**  
The Yonaguni Monument is a large underwater rock formation discovered off the southern tip of Yonaguni Island (Japan) in the East China Sea. Its unusual geometric appearance has sparked debate over whether it is a man‑made structure (possibly an ancient monument) or a natural geological feature that has been misinterpreted.

---

### Confirmed Facts  

| Category | Details |
|----------|---------|
| **Location** | Off the coast of Yonaguni Island, southernmost island of Japan, coordinates ≈ 24°20′ N, 123°14′ E. |
| **Depth** | The main platform lies at a depth of roughly **25 m (≈ 80 ft)** below sea level. |
| **Discovery** | First reported by Japanese diver **Kihachiro Aratake** in **1985**. |
| **Initial Scientific Study** | Marine geologist **Masaaki Kimura** (University of the Ryukyus) began systematic documentation in **1986** and has been the most prominent proponent of an artificial origin. |
| **Rock Composition** | The structure consists of **sandstone and shale**, typical sedimentary rocks of the region. |
| **Geological Setting** | Part of a limestone plateau that was uplifted by tectonic activity and later submerged as sea level rose after the last glacial maximum (~6,000 years ago). |
| **Legal Status** | Not designated as a protected site or UNESCO World Heritage location by the Japanese government or UNESCO. |
| **Archaeological Finds** | No artifacts (tools, pottery, inscriptions) have been recovered that would definitively prove human construction. |
| **Accessibility** | The depth and strong currents limit extensive excavation; most observations are from scuba diving and remote sensing. |

### Scientific Explanation (Natural‑Formation Perspective)  

1. **Tectonic Uplift** – The Yonaguni area lies near the Ryukyu trench. Tectonic forces have raised former coastal limestone plateaus.  
2. **Sea‑Level Rise** – After the last glacial maximum (~6,000 years ago), global sea level rose ~120 m, submerging formerly exposed rock.  
3. **Erosional Shaping** – Wave action, currents, and chemical weathering can carve sandstone and shale into angular blocks and stepped surfaces.  
4. **Joint and Fracture Propagation** – Natural fissures in the rock can produce straight‑looking edges that resemble architectural features.  
5. **Sedimentary Facies** – The rock’s composition (sandstone/shale) is prone to differential erosion, creating the “platform” and “steps” seen today.  

These processes, acting over thousands of years, can produce geometric patterns without any human intervention.

### Evidence  

| Evidence Type | Description | Interpretation |
|---------------|-------------|----------------|
| **Sonar & Photogrammetry** | High‑resolution sonar maps (1995‑2000) and 2022 3‑D models show large, flat surfaces and right‑angle corners. | Supports both natural joint planes and possible artificial shaping. |
| **Rock Composition Analysis** | Samples of sandstone and shale match regional geology; no exotic materials. | Consistent with natural formation. |
| **Absence of Tool Marks** | No microscopic striations, polish, or cut marks observed on exposed surfaces. | Weakens claim of deliberate stone‑working. |
| **Lack of Associated Artifacts** | No pottery, tools, or inscriptions recovered during limited diving expeditions. | Makes a functional monument unlikely. |
| **Geological Context** | The site lies within a known uplifted limestone plateau that was submerged ~6,000 years ago. | Provides a plausible natural origin timeline. |
| **Morphometric Comparison** | Similar stepped formations exist on nearby coastal cliffs (e.g., Cape Inubō). | Suggests a common erosional process. |

*Strongest evidence for a natural origin is the geological setting, rock composition, and lack of unambiguous human artifacts. The artificial‑origin hypothesis relies primarily on visual similarity to known architectural forms.*

### Competing Theories or Explanations  

| Theory | Proponents | Core Claims | Supporting Points | Counter‑Points |
|--------|------------|-------------|-------------------|----------------|
| **Artificial Monument** | Dr. Masaaki Kimura (University of the Ryukyus), some independent divers | The structure is a man‑made pyramid‑like platform with a “torii gate” and possible carvings, built 2,000–3,000 years ago by a prehistoric Japanese culture. | Visual resemblance to stepped pyramids; reported right angles; hypothesis of ancient ritual site. | No artifacts; no tool marks; dating is indirect; many geologists attribute features to natural joint planes. |
| **Natural Erosional Formation** | Mainstream archaeologists, geologists (e.g., members of the Japanese Geological Society) | The shape results from tectonic uplift, sea‑level rise, and differential erosion of sandstone/shale. | Consistent with regional geology; similar formations elsewhere; lack of human modifications. | Does not fully explain some apparent “architectural” features (e.g., the torii‑like shape). |
| **Hybrid Modification** | A minority of researchers | Natural rock was partially modified by humans (e.g., smoothing of surfaces) but not fully constructed. | Some minor surface irregularities could be anthropogenic; the site’s location near ancient trade routes. | Evidence for modification is inconclusive; no definitive human activity markers. |

---

### What Is Still Unknown  

1. **Precise Age** – No direct dating method (e.g., radiocarbon) has been applied to the rock itself; age estimates are based on sea‑level models. |
2. **Purpose / Function** – If human‑made, the monument’s ritual, ceremonial, or utilitarian purpose remains speculative. |
3. **Extent of Human Modification** – It is unclear whether any surfaces were intentionally shaped or merely enhanced by natural processes. |
4. **Buried Features** – Limited excavation means any hidden chambers, carvings, or associated structures remain undiscovered. |
5. **Cultural Affiliation** – No associated archaeological sites have been identified that could link the formation to a known ancient culture. |

---

### Common Myths / Misinformation  

| Myth | Reality |
|------|----------|
| **“Yonaguni is the lost city of Atlantis.”** | No evidence supports this claim; Atlantis is a legendary island described by Plato, and its location is not linked to Yonaguni. |
| **“The monument is a perfect pyramid built by ancient aliens.”** | The structure’s geometry is approximate and can be explained by natural erosion; there is no credible evidence of extraterrestrial involvement. |
| **“The torii gate is a precisely cut stone gateway.”** | The torii‑like shape is a natural rock outcrop; no tool marks or construction joints have been documented. |
| **“Radiocarbon dating proves the site is 10,000 years old.”** | No radiocarbon dating of the monument exists; age estimates are indirect and based on geological models. |
| **“The Japanese government has declared it a World Heritage site.”** | Yonaguni is **not** listed on UNESCO’s World Heritage List, nor has it received any special protection status. |
